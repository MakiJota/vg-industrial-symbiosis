Regional industrial symbiosis in Västra Götaland County — results (2022)

This report applies the same core method as the companion Skaraborg study to all 49 kommuner in Västra Götaland County. Unless stated otherwise, figures refer to the baseline scenario (MAESTRI plus reviewed W2RKG matching, plausibility exclusions and corrected manufacturing scaling) in the mass regime. Explore the interactive county report for kommun, sub-region, industry, product and scenario detail. See also the method and limitations and the Skaraborg case.

  • Waste (10.7 Mt). Mining and quarrying (B) gives 5.6 Mt, construction (F) 2.4 Mt, waste management (E) 1.2 Mt and manufacturing (C) 1.1 Mt. Göteborg (3.1 Mt), Mölndal (1.1 Mt), Lidköping (0.75 Mt) and Stenungsund (0.72 Mt) generate the most. Mineral fuels make up 10.5 Mt of D and 85 bn SEK of its value. The scenario no_energy_inputs removes CN 27 from demand. It is the better reference for what waste can replace, because crude oil cannot be replaced by any industrial waste in the matching databases.

  • Greedy reaches the LP optimum at the regional scale (592 = 592 kt), as in Skaraborg. The kommun two-stage allocation (2b) recovers 99.5 % of it.
  • Exchange between kommuner nearly doubles local use. Intra-kommun exchange gives 324 kt. Exchange within each sub-region adds 144 kt (2c), and exchange across the whole county adds a further 121 kt (2b). Of the 2b flows, 55 % stay within a kommun.
  • Firm-to-firm links are long. The mean exchange distance is 76 km (top-down basis; 64 km bottom-up), against 34–36 km in Skaraborg. The nearest compatible buyer of many streams is in another sub-region.
  • Waste replacing fuels. About 135 kt of waste replaces fuels in the baseline, and those pairs carry most of the value (§6). Without CN 27, the substituted value falls from 3.0 to 1.2 bn SEK, but its share of the (fuel-free) input bill rises to 8.5 %, close to Skaraborg's 8.7 %.

What is substituted (baseline, 2b): crushed stone from quarry overburden (117 kt), fuel wood from bark, wood waste and pallets (100 kt), petroleum oils and gas from oily wastes (132 kt, see §6), soil → salt (49 kt, see §6), concrete articles from construction mixtures (46 kt), limestone from concrete (29 kt), and recovered paper (17 kt). Recovered paper, aluminium scrap and recovered-fibre pulp are small in tonnes but among the most valuable per tonne.

  • Göteborgsregionen is the county's supplier. It sends 160 kt to the other sub-regions and receives 19 kt. Its main exports are 63 kt to Fyrbodal (oily wastes to the Lysekil refinery), 61 kt to Skaraborg (minerals, wood) and 37 kt to Sjuhärad.
  • Sjuhärad gains the most from the county. Its demand met by waste rises from 26 % on its own to 66 % in the county, mostly from Göteborgsregionen and Fyrbodal.
  • Skaraborg is the most self-contained sub-region. On its own it uses 8.2 % of its waste (165 kt), which reproduces the Skaraborg study exactly (consistency check). Inside the county it uses only 6.2 % (124 kt), because larger waste streams from Göteborgsregionen (61 kt) take part of its own demand first. Its demand met by waste still rises to 39.5 %. The greedy rule serves the largest waste first, so it is a ranking, not a market. Whether Göteborg's surplus should displace Skaraborg's own is a policy question, not a model result.
  • Flows between sub-regions are 37 % of the county's exchanged tonnes (219 of 589 kt) and 33 % of the value. Without CN 27, Göteborgsregionen's oily wastes no longer go to the Lysekil refinery (Göteborgsregionen → Fyrbodal drops from 63 to 12 kt). Flows between sub-regions are then 34 % of the tonnes but only 21 % of the value: what crosses sub-region borders is mostly bulky, low-value minerals and wood.

Against what the county actually uses, not just what it imports, demand stops being the limit. Waste could replace 6–18 % of material use, depending on whether quarry waste and fuels are counted. Only lorry freight is in the survey. Göteborg's port, rail and pipeline flows are not, so inflows and outflows of the county are understated, more so than for Skaraborg.

6. Caveats specific to the county

  1. Three LLM-extension pairs are implausible at county scale and should be added to 00_data/match_exclusions.csv, or reviewed. They moved almost nothing in Skaraborg because the demand was not there:

    • 130507 oily water from separators → 2710 petroleum oils: 75 kt, 704 MSEK. The stream is mostly water.
    • 160708 wastes containing oil → 2711 petroleum gas: 47 kt, 871 MSEK.
    • 170504 soil and stones → 2501 salt: 49 kt.

    Together they are 171 kt (29 %) of the baseline tonnes and 1.6 bn SEK (54 %) of its value. Excluding them changes Skaraborg's baseline by 2.3 kt. The no_energy_inputs scenario already removes the first two.

  2. Petroleum support services get the mining beta. NACE 0910 has 63 workers in VG (Stenungsund 33, Göteborg 26), mostly service firms around the chemical cluster and refineries. With beta_B = 11,504 t/worker they generate 0.72 Mt of "mining waste". This is the same flaw as the quarry beta (one B intensity for all of 05–09). The quarry_beta* scenarios rescale NACE 08 but not 09.

  3. Quarry waste: 4.8 Mt modelled, against an SGU-implied quarry output of 15.9 Mt for the county's 421 quarry workers (30 %). The overestimate is about 3–10×, as in Skaraborg.

  4. The firm registry covers sectors A–E only. It includes 92 % of manufacturing workers and none in construction (F, 2.4 Mt of waste). F waste therefore reaches the firm scale only through the "not covered" totals.

  5. Ten firm locations were imputed because coordinates were missing or more than 15 km outside their registered kommun. Firm identifiers and underlying location records are not included in this published report.

7. Priorities for further work

  • Review the three candidate waste-product matches described in §6.1.
  • Use no_energy_inputs (or exclude CN 27 everywhere) as the county's reference for imported demand, and report CN 27 separately.
  • Give NACE 09 its own intensity, or fold it into C/services, as was done for quarries.
